He spins into the attack, every fiber in his body screaming, veins bulging beneath his skin. Inside the hollow in his chest, the red lighter accelerates, roaring like an engine at full throttle.

He channels everything into a single devastating strike.

Flames erupt along his right arm, racing from shoulder to fist until the entire limb is engulfed in fire.

“AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAHHHHHH!!!”

His roar tears through the air as his fist slams forward. His wings ignite, unfurling behind him in a blazing halo as sparks and embers whirl through the air.

KLINK!!

The missile’s nose caves in like a crushed soda can, metal buckling and twisting under the force, fragments of armor bursting outward.

The impact knocks it off course, spinning it violently as it flies away—

BOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOM!!!

It detonates near them instead, the shockwave tearing through the ground and hurling the car further across the debris-strewn road.

His arm is gone—torn apart by the blast.

Inside the car, the radio sparks and burns. Bob sits back, laughing in amazement. Fire outside laughs louder, a nosebleed cutting through it as he drags his remaining hand across his face, smearing the blood away.

The chasers lie in ruins behind them, swallowed by a towering cloud of smoke and fire.

Suddenly, the Civic gives way entirely, collapsing in on itself. Metal twists and glass shatters. The wreckage tumbles forward, carried by its own momentum, before grinding to a halt. Dust swirls around them in a thick cloud.

When it finally settles, Fire—now back in his human form, his white T-shirt returned and his arm restored—and Bob lie on the ground amidst the debris. Silence hangs.

Slowly, Fire sits up first, brushing grit from himself. Then Bob follows. They glance at each other—and burst into laughter all over again.

Fire rises to his feet and offers Bob his hand. Bob takes it, and Fire pulls him up as the sun burns low behind them.

“That… was fun,” Bob says, wiping tears from the corners of his eyes.

Bob hesitates for a second. “Sure, why not?”

Bob leans in as Fire lights his cigarette first, cupping the flicker from the breeze with his other hand while its warm glow briefly washes over them, before he lights his own.

“Oh, and Fire,” Bob says, exhaling smoke, “remember: the loop ends—”

“…Where the Spiral begins,” Fire interrupts, a small grin tugging at his face. “Yeah, I know. Thanks, Bob.”
